About

An entrepreneur and researcher, turning theory into scale.

Prof. Aleksandr Farseev earned his Ph.D. in Computer Science from the National University of Singapore, where his research pioneered multi-view user profiling — synthesising fragmented, noisy signals across social networks into coherent, predictive models of human behaviour.

Today he leads SOMIN.ai as Founder & CEO, translating that research into a generative-AI platform for modern marketers. He is a Guest Professor at NUS and INSEAD, a Research Professor at ITMO University, and a Marketing & Advertising Community Leader at the Forbes Technology Council.

The journey · one obedient yes at a time

How God built a movement.

01First encounter

A boy, an accordion, and a prayer for ducks.

Aleks was eight, spending the summer at his grandmother's village in Russia. Hunters brought him along to play the accordion. When the hunt came back empty, the boy offered to pray for ducks. Minutes later his mother walked out of the village — a duck in her hand.

“Every time I have doubts about God, I remember this incident.”Prof. Aleks Farseev
02Meeting God again

Half a world away, God was waiting in Singapore.

Years later, and thousands of miles from that village, Aleks met God again — this time in Singapore, worshipping as part of City Harvest Church. The childhood conviction that “there is a God in control of everything” became a living, gathered faith in a brand-new city.

03The second encounter

He chose his team over his safety — then peace found him at 30,000 feet.

War and mobilisation hit. SOMIN.ai was losing over $150,000 a month and its Russian team. Aleks refused to abandon them and flew back into conscription risk. Only after he had already decided did comfort arrive — C.S. Lewis's The Screwtape Letters, bought in a London Orthodox bookshop, whispering that “all the bad things could not possibly happen at the same time.” Orthodox and charismatic Christianity, meeting to set things right.

04Redemptive tech

Finding his people at Digital Mission Ventures.

SOMIN.ai joined DMV Redemptive Labs — first with Aleks as a cohort member, then as a mentor to other founders. Here, “business as mission” stopped being a phrase and became a practice: building Southeast Asia's redemptive tech ecosystem, turning down work misaligned with God's values, and trusting that setbacks are just divine redirection.

05An invitation

At the FDE conference, the founder said: start one here.

At the Faith Driven Entrepreneur global conference, Aleks met co-founder Henry Kaestner — who challenged him to plant a chapter in Singapore. That single invitation became Faith Driven Singapore.

06A higher bar

Not another networking club — a family of builders.

Aleks looked hard for a co-founder. Plenty of experienced, willing people stepped forward — yet none was the right fit, and he refused to settle. Faith Driven Singapore had to be something rare: a genuine community of Christ-following founders, not a business fellowship where everyone quietly sells to everyone else.

07A new chapter

The chapter began the moment the right people showed up.

With Aidan Chan and Yuh Tyng Hsiao alongside him, Faith Driven Singapore truly launched — and hasn't stopped growing since, now 200+ members strong.

08On the move

We keep changing venues — not because we want to, but because God does.

Gatherings move around the island — Browzwear, Block71, City Harvest, a coffee-shop table with a book in the middle. We don't cling to a place. We follow where God opens the next door.

09Going global

From Taipei to London to Kenya — Faith Driven goes global.

The first overseas group took root in Taiwan — an introduction, a coffee, a book on the table, and Faith Driven crossed its first border; the Taipei group has been thriving ever since. Now a new group is launching at Meta's London office, with another taking shape in Kenya. The movement doesn't chase geography — it follows the builders who are ready to say yes.

“FDE has been going great in TW btw! Thank you for introducing :)”Angie · Taipei
10Your turn

Why not you — in your corner of Singapore, or your company?

Every chapter began with one person saying yes. Somebody in each of those rooms went first.
